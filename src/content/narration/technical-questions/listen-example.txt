Interviewers rarely include a detail by accident. Every constraint is a load-bearing hint.

If they say the array is sorted, that means binary search or two pointers. If they say this runs repeatedly on a server, that means precompute or cache. If they say the values are between zero and thirty two thousand, that means a bit vector fits in memory. If they say you only have ten megabytes, that is the real constraint and the whole problem is about it.

The classic failure is hearing the constraint and then forgetting it ten minutes later. So when you are stuck, the first question is always: have I used everything I was given?

Then draw an example on the board, not in your head. A good example is specific, with real values. It is big enough to show a pattern, because most people draw one about half the size it should be. And it is not accidentally a special case. A perfectly balanced tree, or an array that happens to be sorted, will hide your bug rather than reveal it.
