Bitwise operators act on each position independently. What happens in one column never affects another. That means a fact you prove for a single bit is automatically true for a whole word, which is why a very short table is worth more here than any memorised expression.

Learn it as three sentences about which operator leaves a bit alone.

AND leaves a bit alone when the mask bit is one, and clears it when the mask bit is zero. So AND is the clearing operator.

OR leaves a bit alone when the mask bit is zero, and sets it when the mask bit is one. So OR is the setting operator.

XOR leaves a bit alone when the mask bit is zero, and flips it when the mask bit is one. So XOR is the toggling operator.

XOR has one extra property that carries a lot of weight. A value XORed with itself is zero, and anything XORed with zero is unchanged. So XOR is a cancellation operator: fold a list together with XOR and everything that appears an even number of times disappears.

The book is blunt on this point, and it is right. Memorising get, set and clear leads to mistakes you cannot recover from mid-interview. Derive them.
