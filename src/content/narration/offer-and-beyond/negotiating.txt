A negotiations class was once asked how much cheaper a car would have to be at the dealership that lets you haggle. The average answer was seven hundred and fifty dollars. People would pay that much to avoid an hour of discomfort. Most of the same students had never negotiated a job offer, which is the same trade at fifty times the price.

So negotiate. Recruiters do not withdraw offers because you negotiated, especially at larger companies where you are not negotiating with your future teammates.

Six rules. Have a viable alternative, because a recruiter improves an offer when they are genuinely worried you will not join. Make a specific ask, since asking for more can be satisfied with a token amount and technically honoured. Overshoot slightly, because you will meet in the middle. Think beyond salary, since companies often move more easily on signing bonus or equity. Use whichever medium you are comfortable in, because asking by email beats not asking. And at a large company, remember that pay is banded by level, so a big jump means arguing for a higher level.

Never bluff about an offer, a number or a deadline. And get every agreed change into the written offer before you accept.
