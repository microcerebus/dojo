The interviews are over and a different set of decisions starts.

Almost every offer has a deadline, usually one to four weeks out. If you are waiting on other companies, ask for an extension. It is a normal request and it is usually granted. Get the offer and every material term in writing: base, bonus structure, equity and its vesting, level, start date, and anything you negotiated. A verbal promise from a recruiter who later leaves the company is worth nothing.

When you decline, give a reason that is non offensive and impossible to argue with. Deciding that a startup is the right environment for you right now cannot be countered by a large company, because they cannot become one. Do not hand them something they can fix unless you want them to fix it. And keep the door open, because you may want this company in a few years, or the people you met may move somewhere you do.

And rejection is noisy evidence, not a verdict. Plenty of excellent engineers interview badly or have an off day, and companies know it. Thank the recruiter, ask when you can reapply, and ask whether there is anything they would suggest working on.
