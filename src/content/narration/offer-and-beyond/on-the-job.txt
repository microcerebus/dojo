Here is a common story. You join, everything is good, and five years later you notice that the last three added nothing to your skills or your resume. Enjoying a job makes it easy to stop noticing that your career has stopped moving.

So set a timeline before you start. Where do you want to be in ten years, and what does this job have to give you to get you there? Then check in once a year and ask what the last twelve months actually added.

Ask for what you want. Some managers actively grow your career. Many take a hands off approach and are simply waiting to be told. If you want more back end work, or more leadership scope, say so plainly.

Build relationships before you need them, because a referral beats an application and your network is what makes the next move possible. Stay in touch with people who leave. A short note a few weeks after they go is what turns a colleague into a contact.

And keep interviewing, about once a year, even when you are happy. It keeps the skill sharp, tells you what the market pays, and builds a connection you may want later. You do not have to take the offer.
