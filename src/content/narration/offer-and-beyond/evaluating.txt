The biggest mistake candidates make is anchoring on base salary. It is the easiest number to compare, which is exactly why it misleads. People routinely accept the offer that was financially worse.

So take the whole package apart. Amortise signing bonuses and relocation over the years you expect to stay. Ask what the annual bonus targets and what it actually paid last year. For public equity, amortise the annual grant. For private equity, ask what percentage of the company it is, not how many shares, and then ask about dilution, vesting, the cliff, the strike price, and how long you have to exercise after you leave, because a ninety day window on options you cannot afford can make a grant worth zero. Then adjust for cost of living and tax, because a thirty percent difference between cities is normal and swamps most salary differences.

Then set the money aside for a moment. What you learn and who you learn it from usually moves your lifetime earnings more than the starting number. So does your manager, the team's growth, and the promotion path. And one thing people almost always overlook: if you leave in three years, are there other companies nearby you would want to work for?
