Testing questions come up even for pure development roles, and they take one of four shapes: test a physical object, test a piece of software, write test code for a function, or debug something that is already broken. All four run on the same method.

On the surface the task is to list a lot of test cases. It is not. Four things are being watched.

Do you know what the thing is for? Testing an online shop, product images in the right place matters. Customers never being double charged matters far more. Say which is which.

Do you see how the pieces fit together? A spreadsheet app is not just open, save and edit. It sits inside an ecosystem, and the seams are where the bugs live.

Are you organised? Reeling off whatever comes to mind guarantees a missed category. Break the thing into parts and work through them.

And is the plan practical? Reinstall the software is not a diagnosis, and a test that needs five real years of use is not a test.

One more rule that applies to all four shapes: assume abuse. Never assume the input or the user will play nice.
