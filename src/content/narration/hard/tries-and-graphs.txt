Word problems at this level are graph and trie problems wearing a disguise, and the question is always which side of the problem to index.

To search one long text for many short patterns, build a trie of the patterns and walk it from every starting position. That is text length times longest pattern, rather than text length times number of patterns.

The longest word made of other words is a recursive check on the suffix, memoised so the same tail is never re-solved. Re inserting spaces to minimise unrecognised characters is a dynamic program over suffixes, where a trie makes enumerating the words that start here cheap. And building a rectangle whose rows and columns are all words is backtracking where, after each row, the trie checks that every partial column is still a valid prefix. Without that check the search never finishes.

Whenever a relation is stated as transitive and symmetric, you are being asked for connected components, and union find is one array and two short functions.

A word ladder is a shortest path, so it is breadth first search, not backtracking. Bucket words under wildcard keys so neighbours are a lookup. And search from both ends: two frontiers of half the depth explore roughly the square root of what one frontier does. When they touch, join the halves, remembering to reverse the backward one.
