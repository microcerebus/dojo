Most interviews open with tell me about yourself, which makes it your interviewer's first impression, and it is entirely within your control.

A chronological arc works for almost everybody. Current role, headline only: one sentence on what you do and where. Then your background, degree and early jobs. Then the path since, and crucially why you made each move, because the reasons are the interesting part. Then your current role in detail, what you own and what you have built. Optionally something outside work, if it is unusual, technical, or says something real. Then wrap up with what you are looking for now and why this company.

Do not announce your achievements. Drop them in. A former manager recruited me to join her startup shows you were good at the last job without claiming it. So does I drove the launch of that service.

Two more rules. Answer the question that was asked before adding context. And when asked about weaknesses, give a real one plus what you do about it, because I work too hard reads as arrogance or an inability to see yourself clearly.
