Public information is context. Your recruiter is the source of truth, and they are usually glad to tell you exactly what is coming, because it is in their interest for you to do well.

Ask how many rounds and how long, what each one covers, whether the coding is on a whiteboard, a shared document or a laptop, which languages are acceptable, whether system design is included and at what depth, what level you are being interviewed for, and when to expect a decision.

Two habits for the day itself. Treat every conversation in the building as part of the loop. The lunch round often carries no formal feedback, but often is not never, and anything memorable travels. Use it for the honest questions you cannot ask a scored interviewer, without switching off.

And do not read anything into your interviewer's manner. A warm one may have written you up badly and a flat one may be your strongest advocate. Some hold a neutral face deliberately, some say good luck to everybody, and some are simply having a bad day. Reading tone mid-loop only costs you focus in the next round.
