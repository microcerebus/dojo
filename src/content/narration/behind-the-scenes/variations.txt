Published company profiles, including the ones in the sixth edition of the book, are historical. Formats change. What is worth keeping is the pattern behind each ritual, because you will meet all of them again under different names.

A dedicated bar keeper brought in from outside the team, with veto power, means one round may feel much harder and much less related to the job. That does not mean you are doing worse in it. A hiring committee that never met you means your interviewers are writing evidence rather than verdicts, and genuine enthusiasm in one write-up can carry a packet further than uniformly middling scores.

Interviewers assigned distinct roles, one behavioural, one or two coding, one design, means the rounds are deliberately non-overlapping, so do not assume the whole loop is algorithms. Hiring by the company rather than by the team means you may accept without knowing where you will land, so ask how placement and internal moves work. Interviewing for one specific team means you should read that team's product and have real opinions about it.

Prepare for the emphasis, never for the trivia.
