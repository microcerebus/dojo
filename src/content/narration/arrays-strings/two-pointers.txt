A huge fraction of array and string problems collapse the moment you use two indices instead of one. Three shapes cover almost all of them.

Converging. One pointer at the start, one at the end, moving towards each other. This needs a rule that lets you discard one end, which is usually sortedness or a symmetry like palindromes.

Fast and slow. Both move forward at different rates, or one lags the other by a fixed gap. That gives you midpoints, kth from the end, and cycle detection.

One pointer per input. Two sorted arrays walked together, each advancing only when its value is behind. That is the merge step of merge sort.

And know why converging pointers are correct. On a sorted array, if the sum of the two ends is too small, then no partner smaller than the right-hand value can rescue the left-hand one, so that element is finished. Each comparison eliminates a whole row of the brute force grid. That argument is the proof, and it is what the interviewer wants to hear.
