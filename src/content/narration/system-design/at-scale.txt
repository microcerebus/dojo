Sometimes you are asked for one algorithm that has to work at scale, not a whole system. There is a four step version. Ask questions. Make believe it all fits on one machine and solve that. Get real: how much does fit, and what breaks when you split it. Then solve those problems, and expect the fixes to create new ones.

Take deduplicating ten billion URLs. In memory it is a hash set. In reality, four terabytes. So hash each URL into one of four thousand files and process them one at a time, or send each to one of four thousand machines and process them in parallel. Faster, but now you depend on four thousand machines behaving. A bloom filter shrinks the memory further at the cost of false positives.

A few of the chapter answers worth carrying. For the social graph, bidirectional breadth first search: searching from both ends turns k to the q into k to the q over two. For a path of four with a hundred friends each, twenty thousand nodes instead of a hundred million.

For a cache, a hash map plus a doubly linked list. Constant lookup, constant eviction.

And poke holes in your own design. Unprompted.
