Big O is a rate of growth, not a stopwatch. The only question it answers is: if the input doubles, how much more work do you do?

That is why an O of n algorithm can genuinely beat an O of one algorithm on real inputs. O of one means the cost does not grow with n. It does not mean fast.

The ladder worth memorising: constant, logarithmic, linear, n log n, quadratic, then exponential and factorial. Logarithmic means the problem halves each step. Quadratic means you touch every pair.

Two warnings. First, runtime is not multiple choice. Square root of n is a real answer, and so is O of a log b. Derive it, do not guess the nearest familiar label. Second, academics separate big O, big omega and big theta, but industry has merged them. In an interview, O means the tightest bound you can state, so calling a single scan quadratic will be marked wrong.
